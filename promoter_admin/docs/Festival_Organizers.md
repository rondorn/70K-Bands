# Open Metal Fest Suite for Festival Organizers

The Open Metal Fest Suite is a free, open-source set of tools designed specifically for metal festivals.

At its core is a dedicated mobile app for each supported festival, backed by an Admin Suite that allows festival organizers or volunteers to maintain the festival information used by the app.

The goal is simple: provide attendees with a useful festival app without requiring the festival to purchase, develop, publish, or maintain its own software platform.

**The Open Metal Fest Suite is free for both festival organizers and attendees. There are no software licensing fees and no per-user fees.**

## What the Attendee App Does

The mobile app is designed around the way people actually use information at a multi-day, multi-stage metal festival.

Attendees can:

- Browse the complete festival lineup
- Research bands through links to resources such as Metal Archives, YouTube, Wikipedia, Bandcamp, and official sites
- Rank bands as Must See, Might See, or Won't See
- Build a personal festival schedule
- Quickly see conflicts between bands they want to watch
- Receive notifications before bands on their schedule perform
- Receive festival announcements and schedule updates
- Share and import selections between devices
- View lineup and attendee statistics
- Continue using core festival information when network access is limited or unreliable

The apps are native applications for iPhone/iPad and Android rather than mobile websites.

The project has grown out of more than a decade of development and real-world use at metal festivals.

## What Festival Organizers Get

The Open Metal Fest Admin Suite provides tools for maintaining the festival-specific information used by the attendee app.

Depending on how involved a festival wants to be, organizers can manage:

- Band lineup
- Band descriptions and festival-specific information
- Performance schedules
- Festival announcements and notifications
- Schedule changes and other updates
- Band images and logos
- Publishing of updated festival data
- Reporting and attendee statistics

The Admin Suite is available for macOS, Windows, iPhone, and iPad.

Maintaining festival information does not require modifying source code or building a new version of the mobile application.

## How the Project Works

Open Metal Fest is open source, but festivals are not expected to build, publish, or maintain their own version of the software.

The normal model is for festival organizers to work with the Open Metal Fest maintainers to configure a dedicated version of the attendee app for their festival.

The Open Metal Fest maintainers are responsible for the software itself, including:

- Configuring the festival-specific version of the app
- Publishing and maintaining the attendee apps
- Releasing software updates and new features
- Maintaining compatibility with current versions of iOS and Android
- Maintaining the shared infrastructure used by the apps
- Addressing software bugs and other technical issues

Festival organizers and volunteers are primarily responsible for the festival-specific information used by the app, including:

- Lineup
- Band information and descriptions
- Band identification and missing artwork when necessary
- Performance schedules
- Festival announcements
- Changes to festival information as the event approaches

How that festival data is maintained can vary. Festival staff can manage it directly through the Admin Suite, volunteers can maintain it, or responsibility can be shared.

The intent is to keep the technical side of operating a mobile app with the people maintaining the Open Metal Fest software while allowing the people closest to each festival to maintain the information specific to that event.

In simple terms: **we maintain the software; you and the festival community maintain the festival.**

## How Much Work Does the Festival Have to Do?

That depends on how involved the festival wants to be in maintaining its data.

There are several ways a festival can participate.

### Organizer Managed

Festival staff can use the Admin Suite directly and maintain their own lineup, descriptions, schedule, and announcements.

This gives the festival direct control over the information published through the app.

The Open Metal Fest maintainers continue to handle the application itself, including publishing, software updates, infrastructure, and technical maintenance.

### Shared

Festival staff and Open Metal Fest volunteers can divide responsibility for festival data.

For example, volunteers might maintain the lineup and band information while the festival maintains schedule information or sends important announcements.

The exact division can be based on what makes sense for the festival and the people involved.

### Community Managed

Festival data can also be maintained by volunteers using information the festival already publishes publicly.

In this model, the festival does not need to perform the day-to-day data entry, but some cooperation is still helpful — particularly with smaller, local, or less-established bands.

Two areas commonly require help from the festival:

- **Identifying the correct band.** Several active bands may share the same or similar names, and an announcement alone may not provide enough information to determine which one is actually performing. A link to the band's official website, Bandcamp, Metal Archives page, social media account, or another authoritative source is usually enough to resolve this.
- **Providing missing band logos.** Logos for established bands are generally easy to locate, but usable artwork for smaller or local bands is often difficult or impossible to find publicly. When that happens, the festival may need to provide a logo or help obtain one from the band.

This does not mean organizers need to assemble a complete information package for every band. Most lineup information can be researched and added without assistance.

What is most helpful is filling the gaps that cannot be reliably resolved from public information: confirming exactly which band was booked and providing artwork when it is otherwise unavailable.

Once those questions are resolved, Open Metal Fest volunteers and tools can handle the remaining research and data entry.

This allows a festival to have an accurate, maintained app without requiring its staff to take on another ongoing administrative task.

## What Does It Cost?

Nothing.

The Open Metal Fest Suite is free for both festival organizers and attendees.

There are:

- No software licensing fees
- No per-attendee fees
- No charge for organizers to use the Admin Suite
- No charge to download the attendee apps
- No required subscription to the Open Metal Fest Suite
- No advertising requirement

The project is open source and is not intended to become another commercial festival-app service.

Festivals retain control of their festival information, and the source code for the software is publicly available.

## Why a Dedicated Festival App?

For a single-stage event, a mobile website and a posted running order may be all that is needed.

The Open Metal Fest Suite is primarily intended for festivals where things become more complicated: multiple stages, multiple days, overlapping performances, large lineups, and attendees making decisions about which bands they want to see.

Instead of repeatedly checking a complete running order, an attendee can build a personal schedule around the bands that matter to them.

The app also provides a single place to research unfamiliar bands before the festival.

Once the festival begins, the same app becomes a practical schedule and notification tool.

## One Festival, One App

Open Metal Fest does not use a single app containing hundreds of festivals.

Each supported festival has its own dedicated app, with its own name, branding, lineup, schedule, notifications, and festival information.

An attendee going to a festival does not need to install a general event app, find the correct festival, and then navigate through another layer of menus before getting to the information they actually need. They simply open the app for that festival.

There is also a practical reason for this approach.

We do not intend to add every festival we can find simply to make the list of supported festivals larger.

Before supporting a festival, we need to be confident that we have the organizer involvement, volunteers, or other resources necessary to keep its information accurate and reasonably current. If we cannot properly maintain a festival, we would rather not release an app for it.

That is an important part of what an Open Metal Fest app is intended to represent: if an attendee downloads one, they should be able to trust that it is actively maintained and useful for that festival.

## Designed for Festival Conditions

Festival networks are not always reliable.

Mobile networks can become overloaded when thousands of people gather in one location, and some festivals take place in areas where coverage is limited even under normal conditions.

The attendee apps are designed with that in mind.

Core festival data is stored on the device so attendees are not dependent on a constant network connection just to check the lineup or their schedule.

Network access is still used for things such as updates and announcements, but losing connectivity does not make the basic festival app unusable.

## Attendee Insights

The Suite also collects anonymous aggregate information about how attendees are using the lineup.

For example, organizers can see how bands are being ranked by participating users and how interest changes as new bands are announced.

Public statistics can provide fans with an interesting look at the festival lineup, while more detailed reporting can be made available to the people maintaining the festival.

These statistics are intended to provide another view of attendee interest, not to replace ticketing, attendance, or other festival data.

## Existing Open Metal Fest Projects

### 70K Bands

70K Bands has been developed and used by attendees of 70,000 Tons of Metal for more than a decade.

The app was the original project from which the broader Open Metal Fest Suite developed.

### MDF Bands

MDF Bands provides the same core platform for Maryland Deathfest.

It allows attendees to research the lineup, rank bands, create schedules, receive notifications, and follow lineup statistics.

MDF Bands is an unofficial community project and is not affiliated with Maryland Deathfest or its promoters.

Additional festivals can use the same underlying platform while maintaining their own festival-specific lineup, schedule, information, branding, and app.

## About the Project Maintainers

Open Metal Fest is maintained by members of the metal community, with most of the software development and ongoing technical maintenance currently handled by Ron Dorn.

I'm a longtime metal fan, musician, software developer, and supporter of open-source software. I perform lead vocals and guitar in the San Francisco Bay Area death/thrash metal band Absolute Darkness and have spent many years attending metal festivals.

Open Metal Fest grew out of combining those interests with a practical problem I experienced as a festival attendee.

The original 70K Bands app was created to make it easier to research a large festival lineup, decide which bands I wanted to see, and manage a personal schedule once the festival started. Continued use by other attendees eventually turned that personal project into something much larger.

The goal with Open Metal Fest is not to build a commercial festival-app business. I want to contribute something useful to the metal community while building a successful open-source project.

For attendees, that means providing tools that make discovering bands, planning a festival, and navigating the event easier.

For festival organizers, it means providing a practical mobile app and the tools needed to maintain it without adding another software licensing expense.

For the project itself, success means having an open-source platform that is actively used, maintained, improved, and supported by the community.

## Open Source

The Open Metal Fest Suite is released as open-source software under the GNU General Public License (GPL).

A festival with the necessary technical resources can use the source code and maintain its own version in accordance with the terms of the license.

That is an option, but it is not the primary way the project is intended to work.

The normal model is for festivals to work with the Open Metal Fest maintainers. We maintain and publish the software while festival organizers and volunteers maintain the festival-specific information.

Keeping the project open source ensures that the software remains available to the community and that festivals are not locked into a proprietary platform simply because they chose to use Open Metal Fest.
